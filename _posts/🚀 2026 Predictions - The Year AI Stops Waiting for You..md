If you thought the last two years were fast, buckle up. According to Fortune 500 AI Advisor [Allie K. Miller](https://www.linkedin.com/in/alliekmiller/), the next 12 months are set to feel like five years of progress.

We are moving away from the era of "chatbots" and into the era of the **"Everything Machine"**. The roadmap for 2026 isn't just about AI getting "smarter", it’s about AI becoming an autonomous teammate that acts on your behalf.

Strategy to prepare for right now:

• **Build Context Systems:** Focus on building organizational knowledge bases for AI to access, rather than just keeping a library of prompts.

• **Establish Agent Protocols:** Define "always do," "ask first," and "never do" boundaries to manage AI autonomy.

• **Measure Value, Not Adoption:** Instead of counting seat licences, organisations should measure reduced response times or the speed of creating new business lines.

• **Train on Evaluations:** The most critical skill will be **judging the quality** of AI output rather than just knowing how to use the tools.

• **Prepare for the platform wars.** Your favorite SaaS tool might become an AI feature. Your workflows might consolidate into a single AI interface. Build flexibility now.

As Miller warns, the gap between AI leaders and laggards will widen dramatically. 
2026 will separate people and organizations into “actual superusers”, “surface users”, and “non-users”.
Which side will you be on? 

The answer will define your competitive edge in 2026.

**Slide 1: 2026 AI Predictions Summary: 5 Years of Progress in 12 Months** 

The AI landscape is accelerating. We are moving beyond tools and toward **autonomous teammates**. Here is the strategic breakdown of what to expect in the next year according to Allie K. Miller.

**Slide 2: The Three Primary Themes**

• **Context Engineering and Memory:** The focus is shifting from simple prompting to building systems that help AI remember a user’s work, preferences, and intent to better intuit their needs.

• **Autonomy and Agency:** AI will move beyond responding in chat threads to taking proactive action as a "teammate" rather than a tool for microtasks.

• **Economic Value:** Evaluation metrics are moving from academic intelligence benchmarks to economic impact—measuring whether AI can perform tasks that generate money.

**Slide 3: Technological Breakthroughs**

• **Claude Code and the "Everything Machine":** Claude Code is expected to move beyond developer terminals into all industries. It represents a shift toward an "everything machine"—a single interface that handles complex, multi-step tasks by breaking them down and verifying its own work.

• **Proactive AI over Prompting:** Users will no longer be the bottleneck. AI will ambiently gather context from platforms like Slack and Zoom to **prompt the human** with completed work for review, shifting the human role from "prompting" to **"verifying"**.

• **Scaling Context and Memory:** Context length is predicted to expand by **10x to 100x**. Additionally, "portable memory" may allow a user’s AI context to follow them across different apps, while "merged memory" could allow teams to share a collaborative AI context.

• **World Models:** These systems will move beyond text to simulate physical environments, unlocking potential for robotics and multimodal understanding.

• **Continual Learning:** Progress is expected in AI that "learns on the job," similar to how humans improve through experience.

**Slide 4: Economic and Social Impacts**

• **The User Divide:** A significant gap is forming between **superusers**, who treat AI as a system-level teammate, and **surface users**, who use it for basic tasks like rewriting emails. 2026 is predicted to be the first year where this leads to a noticeable economic gap.

• **New Benchmarks:** New standards like **GDPval** will measure AI’s ability to perform high-GDP-contributing jobs. AI is expected to handle eight-hour work tasks with 80% reliability.

• **Gen Z Workforce Risks:** Job loss from AI may hit Gen Z the hardest, potentially driving more young people toward the gig economy and entrepreneurship.

• **Geopolitical Shifts:** China is expected to continue dominating the open-source AI market.

**Slide 5: Strategic Actions for 2026**

• **Build Context Systems:** Focus on building organisational knowledge bases for AI to access, rather than just keeping a library of prompts.

• **Establish Agent Protocols:** Define "always do," "ask first," and "never do" boundaries to manage AI autonomy.

• **Measure Value, Not Adoption:** Instead of counting seat licences, organisations should measure reduced response times or the speed of creating new business lines.

• **Train on Evaluations:** The most critical skill will be **judging the quality** of AI output rather than just knowing how to use the tools.

• **Prepare for the platform wars.** Your favorite SaaS tool might become an AI feature. Your workflows might consolidate into a single AI interface. Build flexibility now.
